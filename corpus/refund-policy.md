# Refund policy

This is the policy every other refund article refers back to. It sets out what is always
refunded, what is refunded on request, what is not refunded, and where a human makes the call.

## Always refunded, no time limit

- **Duplicate charges.** Two settled invoices for the same amount and date. Refunded in full. See
  billing-duplicate-charge and refund-duplicate-charge.
- **Charges after a confirmed cancellation.** If you cancelled and were billed anyway, that is
  our error and it is refunded in full. See refund-cancellation.

Neither of these is subject to the 14-day window below.

## Refunded within 14 days

We refund in full within 14 days of a charge if the workspace has not been used meaningfully in
that period. "Meaningfully" is a judgement a human makes, and in practice it means a workspace
that was set up and then not used: a handful of sign-ins, no significant content created, no
large uploads, no sustained API traffic.

This is the reason we do not issue refunds automatically from the product. Almost every request
needs someone to look at the plan, the period and the usage - see refund-how-to-request.

## Not refunded by default

- **Downgrades.** These produce account credit, not money back. See refund-downgrade-credit and
  account-downgrade-plan.
- **Unused seats.** Removing a member mid-period credits the unused days against the next
  invoice; it does not pay cash back. See billing-seat-pricing.
- **Annual plans cancelled mid-term.** The plan runs to the end of the term. There is no default
  pro rata refund.
- **The current period when a workspace is deleted.** Deletion cancels the subscription; it does
  not refund the period already paid for. See account-close-workspace.
- **Account credit itself.** Credit is not redeemable for cash and is forfeited if the workspace
  is closed.

## Decided case by case

- **Prolonged outages.** Exceptions to the annual no-refund rule are decided by a support lead.
  Separately, Business and Enterprise customers have a contractual service credit scheme with
  defined thresholds - see refund-outage-credit, which is the route to use first because it is
  quantified and does not depend on anyone's judgement.
- **Billing errors on our side.** An incorrect seat count or a plan change nobody authorised is
  corrected and refunded. Raise it through billing-invoice-dispute.

## How money comes back

Approved refunds go to the original payment method. There is no option to be paid to a different
card or a bank account, because the refund is a reversal of the original payment rather than a
new one. Card refunds take 5-7 business days and up to 10 with some banks; SEPA reversals take
3-5. See refund-timeline.

You receive an email confirmation and a credit note when the refund is issued, and the invoice
status changes to `refunded` in Billing > Invoices.

## Edge cases and gotchas

- The 14 days run from the charge date on the invoice, not from the date you noticed it.
- Partial refunds of a period are not standard. We either refund the charge or we do not.
- A refund does not cancel the subscription. Cancel separately or the next renewal happens as
  normal.
- Filing a chargeback instead of asking suspends the workspace for up to 90 days and forfeits the
  faster route entirely - see billing-dispute-chargeback.
- Tax is refunded with the charge where it was collected.
- Enterprise contracts may set different terms; the contract wins over this page.

## Troubleshooting

**Symptom: refused a refund on an annual plan you stopped using in month two.**
That is the default position. Cancel to prevent the next renewal, and ask about the outage
exception only if there was a genuine service problem.

**Symptom: you were told it is credit, not a refund.**
The charge was a downgrade or a seat removal. See refund-downgrade-credit.

**Symptom: the 14 days passed by a day or two.**
Ask anyway with the invoice ID and the circumstances. A human reads it.

of the categories above, say so plainly - the exceptions are decided by people, not by this page.
